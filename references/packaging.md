# Packaging and labels

A pack is judged in three seconds on a shelf or counter, handled for a minute at home, and printed by a machine that forgives nothing. This file covers boxes, labels, pouches, cups, bags with print, and the files a converter can run. Read `print.md` too: bleed, type on paper and checking files apply here, and this file adds what is specific to packs.

## 1. Before designing

Ask what the files don't already answer, as fact questions (no recommendation; say what you'll assume if skipped):

- **The product:** what it is, its size and weight, how many variants (flavours, sizes), and whether it's food, cosmetics or something regulated.
- **Where it's sold and how it's seen:** a kiosk counter seen from a metre away, a supermarket shelf next to imports, a delivery bag, a gift box opened at home. Photograph or ask for a photo of the real place.
- **The printer or converter, and their method:** digital (short runs, CMYK only), offset (boxes, spot colours possible), flexo (labels, films, cups, corrugated), screen (bags, simple marks). The method decides colours, fine detail and minimum sizes.
- **The dieline:** always the converter's own file for the real structure and material. A generated one is for concepts only (section 3).
- **The material:** white board, kraft, corrugated, clear or metallised film, paper bag, glass jar with a paper or film label.
- **The information:** who supplies the legal text, ingredients, dates format, barcode number and certifications, and in which languages.
- **Budget for finishes:** foil, spot UV, emboss, soft-touch, a window. Each one is a choice with a cost.

Record the answers under "Packaging" in DESIGN.md. Finish the inventory in `directions.md` section 2 with the product itself: its colour, texture, origin, the way it's made, the numbers only it has (altitude, harvest month, grams, hours of baking).

## 2. Formats and what to call them

Using the right names saves rounds with factories. The FEFCO code (corrugated) and ECMA code (folding cartons) identify standard structures.

| Family | Common formats | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Folding carton | reverse tuck end (RTE), straight tuck end, auto-lock / crash-lock bottom, tray + sleeve, pillow box, gable box with handle | most retail boxes, sweets, cosmetics; board weight and grain matter |
| Rigid box | two-piece lid and base, drawer, book-style with magnet | premium gifts; wrapped paper hides the board |
| Corrugated | mailer box (FEFCO 0427 family), shipping box (FEFCO 0201 regular slotted case) | delivery and e-commerce; print is coarser, bleed often larger |
| Labels | rectangle, wrap-around, round or oval, neck, seal or sticker, sleeve | jars, bottles, tubs; the container's curve limits the label |
| Flexible | stand-up pouch with zip, flat pouch, sachet, flow wrap | films, flexo or digital; seals and gussets eat space |
| Food service | cups and lids, paper bags, burger and pizza boxes, sandwich wedges | grease, heat and wet hands; printing often one or two inks |

## 3. The dieline

- **Get the converter's dieline** for the exact structure, size and material, and build the artwork on it. Board thickness changes panel sizes, so a dieline from another job or the internet will not fit.
- **For concepts only,** generate a starting dieline (templatemaker.nl and GoVisually's free dieline generator export PDF, SVG and DXF) or draw a simple one in SVG, label it "concept dieline, not for production" and replace it before final files.
- **Layers and lines** (in the file the converter gets):
  - cut: solid line; crease/fold: dashed; perforation: dotted;
  - the dieline in a named spot colour (for example `Dieline`), set to overprint, on its own top layer, never printed;
  - bleed past every cut edge: 3 mm usual, more for corrugated or when the converter asks;
  - safe area: text, logos and barcodes at least 3 to 5 mm inside cuts and away from folds (more on corrugated); glue flaps and glue areas free of ink and varnish when the converter asks.
- **Panel orientation:** tuck flaps and the bottom often print upside down relative to the front. Label each panel (front, back, left, right, top, bottom) in a non-printing layer, and check by printing at 100%, cutting and folding a paper prototype.
- **Across folds:** backgrounds and patterns run continuously across folds; type and faces don't sit on a fold. Expect up to 1 mm of fold drift.
- **Windows:** no artwork under a die-cut window unless it's meant to show through the film.

## 4. The front: hierarchy and the shelf tests

**Order of the main face** (the client can reorder, but propose this first):
1. Brand block (logo in a consistent position across the range)
2. Product name in plain words (what it is: "Mountain honey", not only a brand word)
3. Variant (flavour, size, strength), coded by colour and word
4. One claim or reason to buy, in a few words, only if it's true and provable
5. Net quantity (often required on the main face; check)
6. The product picture: a real photo, an illustration, or a window

**Tests before it's an option:**
- **Three seconds at shelf distance** (about 1 to 1.5 m): brand, product and variant can be told apart. Render the front at the size it will be seen and view it small.
- **Arm's length** (60 to 80 cm): the name and variant are readable without searching.
- **Squint:** one focal point, not five things shouting.
- **In context:** put the front among the competitors' packs from the real shelf or kiosk at the same scale. It should stand out by contrast (colour, structure, scale), not by being louder in the same way.
- **On the phone:** most packs are first seen on Instagram or a delivery app. Check the front as a 300px thumbnail.

## 5. Ranges and variants

- **Fixed across the range:** logo position, grid, typefaces, the structure of the front.
- **Changing per variant:** a colour, a picture, the variant name.
- Colour codes must also work without colour: put the variant name and, if useful, an icon. Some shoppers are colour-blind, and kiosk lighting distorts hues.
- Respect the colour codes shoppers already read in the category when they help, and break them only as a decision the client makes knowingly.
- Show the range side by side in every option, not one pack alone: a range is judged as a block.

## 6. Back and sides: the information

The client supplies and approves the legal content. Claude lays it out clearly and never invents claims (halal, organic, sugar-free, "natural", awards, medical benefits) or certification marks without the client's proof.

**Typical food information** (confirm the current rules for each product and market):
- the product's name, ingredients in descending order with allergens emphasised (bold), net quantity, production and expiry dates, storage conditions, the manufacturer or importer's name and address, country of origin, batch or lot number, and nutrition values where required;
- **Iraq, including the Kurdistan Region:** regulated products need the product name, warnings, instructions and ingredients in Arabic, and the country of origin marked; canned foods carry production and expiry dates. Kurdish is usual on products for the Kurdistan Region and required for some (Iraq's anti-smoking law asks for health warnings in Arabic and Kurdish), and English is optional. The authority is COSQC (Iraq's Central Organization for Standardization and Quality Control); rules change, so the client confirms with their importer, lawyer or the authority. This is not legal advice;
- **for export,** the destination's rules apply (for example the EU's Regulation 1169/2011, or the Gulf's GSO standards).

**Legibility floor:** the EU rule for mandatory food text, an x-height of at least 1.2 mm (0.9 mm when the pack's largest face is under 80 cm²), is a sound floor even where no law sets one. In many sans faces that is about 6.5 to 7 pt. Kurdish and Arabic text is set about 1.1 to 1.15x the Latin size, with open line height. Dark text on a light, plain ground; no light grey, no text over pictures, no reversed hairline type.

**Layout:** a clear block per language, nutrition in a simple table, dates and batch in a reserved blank area if they're printed or stamped later at the factory (ask the size and position of that area).

## 7. Barcodes and QR codes

- **The number comes from GS1.** The client licenses a company prefix from a GS1 office and assigns a GTIN to each variant and size. Never invent a number, never reuse one across variants, and never use a generator's random number on a real pack.
- **Make the symbol** from the client's number with `python scripts/ean13.py <12 or 13 digits> --mag 100 --out barcode.svg`. It checks the check digit (a wrong digit usually means a typo in the brief), and draws the bars at exact millimetre size with the quiet zones.
- **Size:** an EAN-13 at 100% is 37.29 x 25.93 mm including the quiet zones and digits (module 0.33 mm). The allowed range is 80 to 200%; aim for 100% when space allows, because tolerances at 80% are tight. Don't cut the bar height.
- **Quiet zones:** at least 11 modules on the left (3.63 mm at 100%) and 7 on the right (2.31 mm). Nothing printed in them.
- **Colour:** dark bars (black is best; dark blue, green or brown can work) on white or a light, plain ground. Never red, orange or yellow bars; scanners read with red light and can't see them. No bars over a pattern or photo. Barcodes print in one solid ink (100% K), never in rich black.
- **Placement:** on the back, lower right, the same place across the range, on a flat area away from edges, folds, seams and perforations. On flat packs the bars stand upright (picket fence). On tight curves (cups, cans, jars under about 12 cm diameter) turn the barcode so the bars run around the curve (ladder), or both ends won't be visible to the scanner at once.
- **QR codes:** keep a quiet zone of four modules, make it at least about 2 x 2 cm, use dark on light, and test-scan the printed proof with two phones.
- Test-scan the printed proof with a phone app and, when possible, the shop's own scanner.

## 8. Inks, finishes and materials

- **Colour build:** CMYK for digital and most offset; brand-critical colours as spot inks (Pantone) when the run and press allow (common in flexo labels, bags and offset boxes). Ask the printer for their colour profile and total ink limit, and proof brand colours on the real material.
- **Black:** large dark areas in the printer's rich black (a common build is C60 M40 Y40 K100; use theirs). Small text, fine lines and barcodes in 100% K only, so they stay sharp.
- **Fine detail:** typical minimums are 0.25 pt lines (positive) and 0.5 pt (reversed), and about 6 pt type for small print, more for flexo and corrugated. Ask the printer and follow the bigger number.
- **White ink:** on clear film, metallised film and kraft, colours need a white layer under them to stay bright. Draw it as its own spot layer named `White`, and decide with the printer where it goes.
- **Finishes** (foil, spot UV, emboss, deboss, soft-touch areas, varnish-free glue areas) each get their own spot layer named for the finish, set to overprint, with a small finish map in the spec. Thin foil and emboss details fill in; keep them bold.
- **Kraft and uncoated stocks:** inks print darker and duller and there's no true white. Test the palette on kraft before promising it, and design with the brown as a colour.
- **Food contact, grease and wet:** ask the converter which inks and coatings are safe inside and where.
- **Files:** a PDF in the standard the printer asks for (often PDF/X-4 or PDF/X-1a), fonts embedded or outlined if asked, images at 300 ppi at final size, every spot colour named consistently across files.

## 9. Kurdish and Arabic on packs

- **Which language leads the main face** is a decision recorded in DESIGN.md: Kurdish first for local products in the Kurdistan Region, with the required Arabic and optional English placed on the back and sides; an export pack may lead in English.
- **Options:** one face per script (for example Kurdish on the front, English on the back face), or one bilingual face with the leading script larger. Never two scripts in one line.
- **Panel order follows the leading script:** on a wrap label or a multi-panel box led in Kurdish or Arabic, the reading continues to the panel on the left, so the story and the back panel flow right to left.
- All rules from `rtl-kurdish-arabic.md` apply: real Kurdish fonts checked with `glyph_check.py`, no letter-spacing, 1.1 to 1.15x size, digits chosen once, phone numbers and weights in isolated left-to-right runs.
- Legal text is never machine-translated. The client supplies it or approves a native speaker's translation; every string Claude writes goes on the "Text to check" list.

## 10. Mockups and previews

- **Flat artwork first:** each panel at true size on the dieline, shot or exported as PNG for the comparison page.
- **3D preview:** `assets/pack-mockup.html` folds front, side and top images into a rotatable box in the browser (and a flat label wrapped on a cylinder for jars and cups). Fill its CONFIG with the box size in mm and the panel images, then shoot it (`node scripts/shoot.mjs <mockup> <out> --sizes=1200x900`). Show the whole range side by side.
- Free online tools such as Pacdora and Canva's mockups can make photo-style mockups; check their licence before giving them to a client.
- Mockups are labelled as mockups. Never show a generated "photo" as if it were the real product or shelf.
- Before the run, a physical prototype beats any render: print at 100% on paper, cut, fold, put the product in it, and look at it on the real counter.

## 11. Honest and lighter packs

- Use less: smaller boxes, fewer layers, no plastic window if a paper version works, one material where possible (easier to recycle).
- Recycling symbols, FSC marks and "eco" claims only when true and certified. No greenwashing.
- Large solid ink floods cost more and use more ink; a coloured board or kraft can do the same job.

## 12. Checking and handing over

**Preflight before sending files:**
- [ ] Built on the converter's dieline; the dieline layer is a named spot colour, overprinted, and not printing.
- [ ] Bleed on every cut edge; text, logos and barcodes inside the safe area and off the folds.
- [ ] Panel orientation checked on a printed, cut and folded paper prototype.
- [ ] Small text above the floor in every language; no reversed hairlines; legal text proofread by a native speaker of each language.
- [ ] Barcode: correct number and check digit, size 80 to 200%, quiet zones clear, dark on light, 100% K, scan-tested on the proof.
- [ ] Colours as agreed (CMYK, spot names, white and finish layers), images 300 ppi at size, fonts embedded or outlined as asked.
- [ ] Every variant's file matches its variant (names, colours, barcode, net quantity).

**The hand-off folder:**
- `FINAL - Ready to Print/<variant>.pdf` for each variant, plus a dieline-only PDF;
- flat previews (PNG) and 3D mockups for the client's approval and Instagram;
- the editable source files and fonts list;
- a one-page spec per pack: structure and size, material, print method, inks (CMYK and spots), finishes with their layer names, barcode numbers, quantities, and the printer's name;
- what you couldn't check (press colour, the real material, the cutter, scanning in the shop).

## 13. Review checklist

- [ ] The front passes the three-second, arm's-length, squint, in-context and thumbnail tests.
- [ ] The range reads as one family and every variant can be told apart without colour.
- [ ] The back is legible, complete as the client supplied it, and laid out per language.
- [ ] Barcode and QR rules met (section 7).
- [ ] The file follows the converter's specs (sections 3 and 8).
- [ ] No invented claims, certifications or proof.
- [ ] Mockups labelled; a physical prototype recommended before the run.
